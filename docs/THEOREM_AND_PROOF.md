# Theoretical Justification for Conditional Intervention Analysis (CIA)

This document provides a formal theorem and proof sketch suitable for inclusion in the workshop paper. CIA approximates interventional effects via conditional sampling from learned distributions \(P(F_i \mid F_{-i})\).

---

## 1. Setup and Notation

Let \(\mathbf{F} = (F_1, \ldots, F_p)\) denote the feature vector and \(Y\) the outcome. We assume a structural causal model (SCM):

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_i &:= g_i(F_{-i}, U_i), \quad i = 1, \ldots, p, \\
Y &:= h(\mathbf{F}, U_Y),
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(\{U_i\}_{i=1}^p\) and \(U_Y\) are mutually independent exogenous variables (no hidden confounding at the structural level).

For a fixed feature index \(i\), define the **atomic intervention** \(\mathrm{do}(F_i = f_i^\*)\) as replacing the structural equation for \(F_i\) with the constant \(f_i^\*\), holding all other equations fixed.

The **average causal effect (ACE)** of a small perturbation to feature \(i\) at covariate profile \(\mathbf{f} = (f_1, \ldots, f_p)\) is:

\[
\Delta_i(\mathbf{f}) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[Y \mid \mathrm{do}(F_i = f_i + \delta), F_{-i} = f_{-i}\right] - \mathbb{E}\!\left[Y \mid \mathrm{do}(F_i = f_i), F_{-i} = f_{-i}\right],
\]

for infinitesimal \(\delta\), or equivalently the partial derivative of the interventional response surface with respect to \(f_i\).

---

## 2. Assumptions

**A1 (Structural causal model).** Data are generated by the SCM above with independent exogenous noise and no cycles.

**A2 (Local conditional ignorability).** For each feature \(i\),

\[
(Y \perp\!\!\!\perp U_i \mid F_{-i}) \quad \text{under intervention on } F_i \text{ within the observed support.}
\]

Interpretation: given all other observed features, unobserved factors driving \(F_i\) do not directly affect \(Y\) except through \(F_i\). This is the key assumption that makes conditioning on \(F_{-i}\) a valid substitute for blocking backdoor paths when intervening on \(F_i\).

**A3 (Correct conditional model).** The estimated conditional distribution \(\hat{P}(F_i \mid F_{-i})\) converges to the true \(P(F_i \mid F_{-i})\) (in total variation or Wasserstein distance) as sample size increases.

**A4 (Correct outcome model).** The fitted outcome model \(\hat{\mu}(\mathbf{F}) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathbf{F}]\) (regression) or \(\hat{p}(\mathbf{F}) = P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{F})\) (classification) is consistent for the true conditional expectation on the support of \(\mathbf{F}\).

**A5 (Positivity / overlap).** For all \(\mathbf{f}\) in the evaluation set and all counterfactual values \(f_i'\) sampled from \(P(F_i \mid F_{-i} = f_{-i})\), the joint vector \((f_i', f_{-i})\) lies in the support where \(\hat{\mu}\) (or \(\hat{p}\)) is accurate.

**A6 (Smoothness).** \(h\) is differentiable in \(F_i\) at almost all points in the support, so finite-difference approximations converge to the local interventional derivative as perturbation size shrinks.

These assumptions are standard in the interventional ML literature (cf. conditional permutation importance, interventional SHAP, causal permutations). CIA makes them explicit.

---

## 3. Main Theorem

**Theorem (Soundness of CIA under A1–A6).**  
Fix feature index \(i\). Let \(\tilde{F}_i \sim P(F_i \mid F_{-i} = f_{-i})\) be a counterfactual draw for subject with observed \(\mathbf{f}\). Define the CIA finite-difference statistic for that subject:

\[
D_i(\mathbf{f}) \;=\; \frac{\hat{\mu}(f_i', f_{-i}) - \hat{\mu}(f_i, f_{-i})}{f_i' - f_i},
\quad f_i' = \tilde{F}_i,
\]

with the analogous definition for classification using \(\hat{p}\) in place of \(\hat{\mu}\). Let \(\bar{D}_i = \mathbb{E}[D_i(\mathbf{F})]\) where expectation is over subjects and counterfactual draws.

Then, as sample size \(n \to \infty\), counterfactual samples \(K \to \infty\), and perturbation magnitude \(\mathbb{E}[|f_i' - f_i|] \to 0^+\):

\[
\bar{D}_i \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial f_i}\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[Y \mid \mathrm{do}(F_i = f_i), F_{-i}\right]\Big|_{\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{f}}\right],
\]

the average **local interventional effect** of feature \(i\) under the SCM.

For binary \(Y\), the same statement holds with \(\hat{p}\) and the interventional probability surface.

---

## 4. Proof Sketch

### Step 1: From observational conditioning to interventional distribution

Under **A1** and **A2**, for a fixed value of the remaining features \(F_{-i} = f_{-i}\), intervening on \(F_i\) while holding \(F_{-i}\) fixed yields:

\[
P(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(F_i = f_i'), F_{-i} = f_{-i}) \;=\; \mathbb{E}[Y \mid F_i = f_i', F_{-i} = f_{-i}]
\]

when there is no open backdoor path from \(F_i\) to \(Y\) that is not blocked by \(F_{-i}\). **A2** is precisely the condition that rules out such paths via unobserved \(U_i\).

Thus, evaluating the outcome model at counterfactual \((f_i', f_{-i})\) targets the interventional quantity rather than a purely associational one.

### Step 2: Conditional sampling identifies plausible interventions

CIA does not pick arbitrary \(f_i'\); it samples \(f_i' \sim P(F_i \mid F_{-i} = f_{-i})\). By **A3**, these samples lie on the **observed manifold** of feature co-occurrences. This implements a **stochastic soft intervention** that respects the joint structure of \(\mathbf{F}\), analogous to sampling from the conditional in conditional permutation importance (Strobl et al., 2008) and interventional SHAP (Janzing et al., 2020).

For small perturbations, \(\mathbb{E}[f_i' \mid F_{-i} = f_{-i}] = f_i\), so the finite difference estimates a local derivative rather than a large off-manifold extrapolation.

### Step 3: Outcome model substitution

By **A4** and **A5**, replacing \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid F_i = f_i', F_{-i}]\) with \(\hat{\mu}(f_i', f_{-i})\) introduces error that vanishes as the outcome model converges. The CIA estimator is therefore consistent for the interventional contrast computed through \(\mu\).

### Step 4: Aggregation across subjects and folds

CIA averages \(D_i(\mathbf{f})\) over subjects (and cross-validation folds). By the law of large numbers and **A6**, as \(K \to \infty\) (counterfactual samples per subject),

\[
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{\hat{\mu}(f_i^{(k)}, f_{-i}) - \hat{\mu}(f_i, f_{-i})}{f_i^{(k)} - f_i}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\frac{\partial \hat{\mu}}{\partial f_i}(f_i, f_{-i})
\]

almost surely for each \(\mathbf{f}\). Taking expectation over \(\mathbf{F}\) and using **A4** gives the stated limit.

### Step 5: Statistical testing

Cross-validated fold means of per-feature effects are tested with a one-sample \(t\)-test against zero. Under the null of no interventional effect, \(\mathbb{E}[D_i] = 0\); under alternatives, significant effects indicate features whose local interventional perturbations change the outcome beyond sampling noise. This does not prove causality in observational data without **A1–A2**, but it is **sound** given those assumptions.

\(\square\)

---

## 5. Corollaries and Practical Implications

**Corollary 1 (Synthetic identifiability).** When the SCM is known (simulation), **A1–A2** hold by construction. If **A3–A4** are satisfied with sufficient sample size, CIA recovers true causal features with high probability. This justifies presenting synthetic results before ABCD.

**Corollary 2 (Failure modes).** If **A2** is violated (hidden confounding between \(F_i\) and \(Y\) not captured by \(F_{-i}\)), CIA may attribute associational effects to \(F_i\). If **A3** fails (poor conditional model), counterfactuals may be off-manifold. If **A4** fails (misspecified outcome model), effects reflect model artifacts. These are limitations to state explicitly in the paper.

**Corollary 3 (Relation to do-calculus).** CIA implements a **Monte Carlo estimate** of a soft intervention:

\[
\mathbb{E}_{f_i' \sim P(F_i \mid F_{-i})}\!\left[\frac{Y(f_i', F_{-i}) - Y(f_i, F_{-i})}{f_i' - f_i}\right],
\]

which coincides with the ACE under **A1–A6** and small perturbations.

## 6. Suggested Paper Text (short paragraph)

> We formalize Conditional Intervention Analysis as a Monte Carlo estimator of local interventional effects under a structural causal model with local conditional ignorability (Assumption A2). Counterfactual feature values are drawn from the learned conditional distribution \(\hat{P}(F_i \mid F_{-i})\), ensuring perturbations remain on the observed feature manifold. Under correct specification of the conditional and outcome models (A3–A4), Theorem 1 shows that the CIA effect estimate converges to the average local interventional derivative. We first validate this property on synthetic data with known ground truth, then apply CIA to ABCD using FIBE-selected features, excluding ancestry proportions and retaining parent education as a prespecified covariate.
